Re-employment, Earnings Scarring and the Public Cost of AI Displacement

How much does additional time out of work cost Germany’s public budget? I model how AI-related job displacement and re-employment affect public finances over ten years, with supporting analysis of earnings scarring and household income.

Explore employment transitions, benefit spending and lost public receipts. Results are conditional scenarios, not forecasts or estimates of AI’s overall economic impact.

Governing Frontier AI: Organizational Boundaries and the Providers of AI Governance

Sole-authored

Who performs frontier-AI governance? I map 238 documented instances into 99 distinct relationships across ten developers; 58 cross organizational boundaries. The study compares this evidence with 51 commercial specialist providers. Disclosure concentration limits conclusions about the wider market.

Contribution: Original disclosure coding and a comparison of developer-level governance arrangements with venture-oriented provider data.

Estimating Differential Routing Rates in Germany’s Federal Employment Agency

With Aileen Voit, Nafisah Animashaun and Leonard Schmidt

Using monthly administrative statistics, we document disparities in occupational registration and vocational training within Germany’s unemployment-insurance regime. Aggregate differences do not establish algorithmic causation. Submitted to the AuDe workshop proceedings (CEUR-WS).

My contribution: Designed the monitoring framework, built the bounded estimator and wrote the empirical sections.

Current questions

Work in progress

Chinese and US Greenfield FDI over the Global Financial Cycle

With Xiang Li · Work in progress

Do Chinese multinationals respond to the global financial cycle differently from US multinationals? Using project-level greenfield FDI data (fDi Markets, 2003–2023), we compare Chinese and US projects within the same destination, sector and quarter.

Chinese greenfield investment turns out to be more sensitive to global risk: a one-standard-deviation rise in the VIX lowers Chinese projects by about 10% relative to US projects, and the effect is larger before COVID and after 2010.

Local projections with the AEI China Global Investment Tracker show that Chinese investment deals fall sharply in the quarter after large spikes in global risk aversion, and the fall does not persist. Ongoing work examines the role of US presence in destination markets and compares project data with IMF bilateral FDI positions.

  • fDi Markets, 2003–2023
  • Global risk · VIX
