Working paper 2026

Estimating Differential Routing Rates in Germany’s Federal Employment Agency

An epidemiological approach to AI incident monitoring in high-risk labour-market systems · with Aileen Voit, Nafisah Animashaun and Leonard Schmidt · SAIGE Incubator Working Paper

This paper examines differences in how unemployed jobseekers are registered across support categories within Germany’s Federal Employment Agency. Using published monthly statistics, we find that flight-context jobseekers in the SGB III insurance regime are registered for unskilled-tier occupations 1.6 times as often as the comparison group and receive substantive vocational training at half the rate, while SGB II shows parity. Published aggregate data locate the disparity in SGB III but cannot identify whether it reflects the profiling infrastructure, selection into the insurance channel or caseworker practice.

  • Published administrative statistics
  • Bounded estimation
  • Incident monitoring
  • EU AI Act
  • June 2016 – April 2026

Work in progress Sole-authored · IWH Halle

Predicting the Price of Safety: AI Governance Exposure and Venture Financing

Asks whether AI governance and safety considerations shape venture financing for AI and deeptech startups. May 2025 Crunchbase records are linked into firm–round, investor-edge and event-study panels, separating explicit governance disclosure from broader governance exposure — startups in compliance-sensitive domains such as healthcare, finance, cybersecurity, biometrics and autonomous systems. Preliminary event-study evidence shows no immediate funding response around the EU AI Act trilogue, consistent with delayed investor learning.

  • Crunchbase (May 2025)
  • Event study
  • Investor networks
  • Text-based measurement

Work in progress Dissertation chapter Sole-authored · IWH Halle

Multinational Firms over the Global Financial Cycle: Greenfield FDI and the Transmission of US Monetary Policy

Links project-level greenfield investment announcements from fDi Markets to Compustat, building a firm- and project-level panel of US multinationals and their foreign expansions, and asks whether US monetary policy surprises transmit differently through multinationals than through purely domestic firms. A US–China comparison tests whether multinationals headquartered in the two economies adjust foreign investment differently over the global financial cycle.

  • Compustat
  • Monetary policy surprises

Dissertation

2025–2029 (expected) · Leipzig University & Halle Institute for Economic Research (IWH)

Multinational Enterprises and the Transmission of the Global Financial Cycle

Provisional working title

The dissertation studies how shifts in global risk aversion, financing conditions and uncertainty affect firm-level productivity, investment and employment, and whether multinational firms amplify these shocks or absorb them through diversified operations and internal capital markets.

The planned empirical strategy combines firm-level production-function estimation with panel regressions containing firm and time fixed effects. It is designed to distinguish changes in physical productivity from price and demand effects, trace adjustment through labour and capital, and estimate heterogeneous responses by multinational status and financial constraints. Global financial conditions are measured using established indicators of risk aversion, credit conditions and uncertainty, while identification exploits differences in firms’ pre-existing international exposure and balance-sheet characteristics.

01OutcomesPhysical productivity, investment and employment at the firm level.
02IdentificationGlobal financial indicators interacted with predetermined firm exposure.
03ChannelsExternal financing, uncertainty and internal capital reallocation.
