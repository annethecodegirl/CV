Research profile

Empirical AI governance and safety, approached with the tools of applied economics: measuring algorithmic harm in deployed high-risk systems, AI incident monitoring, and compliance under the EU AI Act. Alongside this, work on how governance exposure prices into capital allocation, and on the macro-financial and labour-market effects of AI adoption.

Methodologically, the through-line is causal inference on administrative and firm-level microdata, event studies, and text- and network-based measurement — tools that transfer directly to evaluating deployed AI systems and estimating their downstream effects.

Selected research

Working paper 2026

Estimating Differential Routing Rates in Germany’s Federal Employment Agency

An epidemiological approach to AI incident monitoring in high-risk labour-market systems · with Aileen Voit, Nafisah Animashaun and Leonard Schmidt · SAIGE Incubator Working Paper

Germany’s Federal Employment Agency routes unemployed jobseekers into support tracks of differing intensity. Treating the question as one of administrative surveillance, we estimate exposure and harm separately from published monthly statistics for June 2016 to April 2026. In the SGB III insurance regime, flight-context jobseekers are registered for unskilled-tier occupations 1.6 times as often as the comparison group and receive substantive vocational training at half the rate; SGB II shows parity.

  • Published administrative statistics
  • Bounded estimation
  • Incident monitoring
  • EU AI Act
  • June 2016 – April 2026

Work in progress Sole-authored

Predicting the Price of Safety: AI Governance Exposure and Venture Financing

Asks whether AI governance and safety considerations shape venture financing for AI and deeptech startups. May 2025 Crunchbase records are linked into firm–round, investor-edge and event-study panels, separating explicit governance disclosure from broader governance exposure — startups in compliance-sensitive domains such as healthcare, finance, cybersecurity, biometrics and autonomous systems. Preliminary event-study evidence shows no immediate funding response around the EU AI Act trilogue, consistent with delayed investor learning.

  • Crunchbase (May 2025)
  • Event study
  • Investor networks
  • Text-based measurement

Work in progress Dissertation chapter Sole-authored · IWH Halle

Multinational Firms over the Global Financial Cycle: Greenfield FDI and the Transmission of US Monetary Policy

Links project-level greenfield investment announcements from fDi Markets to Compustat, building a firm- and project-level panel of US multinationals and their foreign expansions, and asks whether US monetary policy surprises transmit differently through multinationals than through purely domestic firms. A US–China comparison tests whether multinationals headquartered in the two economies adjust foreign investment differently over the global financial cycle.

  • fDi Markets
  • Compustat
  • Monetary policy surprises
  • Firm-level panel

Doctoral research

2025–2029 (expected) · Leipzig University & Halle Institute for Economic Research (IWH)

Multinational Enterprises and the Transmission of the Global Financial Cycle: A Firm-Level Analysis for Germany

Provisional working title

The dissertation asks how global financial conditions reach the real economy through individual firms, and whether multinationals’ international production and financing structures amplify exposure to global shocks or instead confer resilience.

The German component uses confidential AFiD firm-level microdata — covering private as well as listed firms, with the price and quantity information needed to estimate physical total factor productivity — through production function estimation and panel regressions with firm and time fixed effects. The greenfield FDI chapter listed under selected research uses a separate fDi Markets–Compustat panel.

  • AFiD microdata
  • Production function estimation
  • TFPQ
  • Panel fixed effects
